Who is doing what?

Customer app

The customer chooses a service, books windows, provides a card and instructions, tracks their own order, and reports an issue.

Driver module

Inside the Partner app. The driver accepts routes, collects and measures laundry, scans handoffs, and delivers with proof.

Workshop module

Inside the same Partner app. The workshop accepts work, scans/counts intake, processes laundry and prepares the clean bag for return.

Backend

The central decision-maker: it checks permissions and rules, stores records, matches partners, tracks custody and calculates money.

Low-code staff workspace

Staff configure the pilot and handle exceptions through secured backend actions. They do not edit the production database directly.

Card/payment gateway

The external service securely collects and validates card details, charges once after pickup, and reports payment/subscription events.

Undecided settings are labels, not extra journey steps.

City, zones/hours, rates/tax, authentication, payment gateway, partner checks, offer timing, capacity and route settings remain D-001 to D-010 inputs. Values must be approved for live use; the general story can still be understood now.

How the price works

At booking: the backend reads the selected service's configured minimum charge and standard pickup-and-return delivery fee. It checks the customer's active subscription entitlement and uses zero delivery fee when the waiver applies.
Spec references: OPS-003; CUS-010; SUB-002; SUB-003

Booking estimate = tax-inclusive service minimum + applicable delivery fee. Display the total, its service/delivery breakdown and the included tax. The measured weight/count is recorded at pickup, so the estimate starts from the configured minimum and the final amount may be higher.
Spec references: CUS-010; PAY-004; SUB-002

After pickup: use the driver’s recorded weight and garment count, together with the price/tax/rounding versions saved on the order. Post the calculation to the ledger, then request exactly one final charge.
Spec references: DRV-007; PAY-003; PAY-004; PAY-005; PAY-007

Baseline serviceService amount before eligible delivery fee
Wash / Dry / FoldHigher of the service minimum and actual pickup weight × weight rate.
Iron / FoldHigher of the service minimum and driver-confirmed pickup ironing count × garment rate.
Full ServiceHigher of the service minimum and (pickup weight × weight rate + confirmed selected ironing count × garment rate).

Add the eligible delivery fee. An active CAD 13.99 subscription waives standard pickup-and-return delivery; laundry charges remain. Show tax-inclusive totals and preserve tax/rule/rounding evidence under approved D-003 values—do not add tax a second time.

What if the driver and workshop count different numbers of garments? The customer is charged after pickup using the driver's recorded weight and confirmed pickup count, according to the selected service's pricing rules. If the workshop records a different count, processing pauses while staff checks the pickup record, workshop count and handoff evidence. The difference does not automatically change the bill or create another charge.

  • The pickup record was wrong and the bill was too high: staff reviews the evidence and can make an approved refund or correction. The original records stay unchanged; the reason and money adjustment are saved separately.
  • The pickup record was correct but garments are missing: staff investigates the missing garments and decides the outcome under the approved incident/claim policy. A lower workshop count alone does not prove that the original bill was wrong.
  • The workshop count was wrong: reconcile the count and keep the original financial calculation unless an approved correction is needed.

A different weight recorded by the workshop is kept for operations. It does not replace the driver's billing weight, change the customer charge or pause processing by itself. Exact refund/correction and count-discrepancy outcomes still need the approved D-013 and D-014 policies.
Spec references: DRV-007; PAY-003; PAY-004; PAY-010; SPV-005; SPV-006; OPS-006; D-013; D-014

CUS-018, CLM-001 to CLM-007

Driver and provider disagree: who controls it?

The driver and provider each submit their own count. Neither silently replaces the other’s value. Authorized staff uses the low-code workspace to resolve the case through the backend.

  1. Detect: preserve driver count and provider count; enter COUNT_DISCREPANCY.
  2. Pause: backend refuses start-processing while the discrepancy is unresolved. A missing provider scan also blocks processing.
  3. Investigate: staff opens the case and compares manifest, custody scans, selected ironing photos and any incident evidence; obtain clarification as needed.
  4. Resolve: staff records confirmed count, reason, evidence, customer impact; backend records resolver/time and validates the command.
  5. Continue: after reconciliation or valid audited resolution, and with custody gates satisfied, the provider may process. Unresolved cancellation/refund outcomes remain D-014 pending.
  6. Money: no automatic second charge. An approved manual financial correction is a new ledger entry.
  7. Weight only differs? log provider weight for operations; driver billing weight stays authoritative and processing does not pause solely for weight variance.
Damage is a different branch from count mismatch.

Questions Q1/Q3 say the workshop or driver contacts the system when damaged clothing appears, and accepts/rejects after communication. The exact incident, acceptance/rejection, custody, cancellation and financial outcome must be written into the Terms/policy. Do not assume a damage incident automatically uses COUNT_DISCREPANCY or requires a routine new condition-photo/toggle step.

Driver navigation: using Google Maps

The driver follows the assigned stop order in Garments Grid and opens directions for one stop at a time. DRV-004 and RTE-012 require links to an approved navigation app. Google Maps is the example here; the navigation-app choice still needs approval.

What the driver does

  1. Accept and start the route. Review the offer and ordered stop list in the Driver module. Starting locks the route. The backend has already planned the stop sequence.
  2. Open the next stop. Read its type, scheduled window, assigned address and instructions. Tap Navigate in Google Maps on that stop.
  3. Follow directions to that location. Garments Grid passes the stop's verified coordinates to Google Maps. With the driver's current location available, Maps can start driving navigation; otherwise it may show a route preview. The driver checks the destination before travelling.
  4. Return to Garments Grid on arrival. Switch back to the Driver module. Opening Maps or reaching the address does not complete the stop. Record the required count, weight, QR handoff or delivery proof there.
  5. Complete the stop and open the next one. Wait for the required backend acknowledgement, or keep any permitted offline work visibly pending for retry. Continue in the assigned sequence; do not skip unresolved custody steps.
  6. Repeat for the clean-laundry return. A return route may have a different driver. That driver navigates to the workshop, scans and collects the clean bag, then navigates to the customer and records delivery proof.

If navigation fails
  • Google Maps is not installed: its universal link opens in a browser. If opening fails, keep the address visible for an approved navigation fallback; leave the stop pending.
  • Location unavailable or wrong destination: check the assigned address and directions before proceeding. Contact staff for an address/route exception; do not mark the stop complete.
  • Road closure or delay: Maps may change the roads to the same destination. Changing the assigned stop order is a separate staff emergency action with audit/version history and driver notification.
  • No connectivity: the cached stop list remains useful. Navigation availability depends on the external app and downloaded coverage. Show pending scan/photo retries clearly; report any unsafe or impossible leg to staff.
Developer notes: navigation link and backend boundaries

Get the ordered, authorized stops from GET /v1/driver/routes/{id}/stops (representative API). Build each navigation link from that stop's verified coordinates using a URL-encoding library:

https://www.google.com/maps/dir/?api=1&destination=LATITUDE%2CLONGITUDE&travelmode=driving&dir_action=navigate

Replace the coordinate placeholders with the assigned stop's values. Omitting origin lets Maps use the device location where available. Send the destination only; exclude customer names, phone numbers, order IDs and bag details. Launching navigation does not update custody, payment or order state.

Google Maps URLs need no Google API key. This is distinct from the backend travel-time matrix used by OR-Tools: its provider and service costs remain pending D-009.
